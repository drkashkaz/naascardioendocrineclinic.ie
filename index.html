GLP-1 Receptor Agonists: Safe Prescribing &
The Risks of Unregulated Use

GLP-1 receptor agonists (GLP-1 RAs) â including semaglutide (OzempicÂ®, WegovyÂ®), tirzepatide (MounjaroÂ®) and liraglutide (SaxendaÂ®) â represent a significant advance in the treatment of type 2 diabetes and obesity. However, the rapid proliferation of online prescribing platforms, compounding pharmacies, and over-the-counter acquisition channels has created a patient safety emergency that demands urgent clinical attention.

What GLP-1 RAs Are â and Are Not

GLP-1 receptor agonists mimic the endogenous incretin hormone glucagon-like peptide 1, stimulating glucose-dependent insulin secretion, suppressing glucagon, slowing gastric emptying, and producing central satiety signals. In clinical trials, semaglutide 2.4 mg (Wegovy) produced mean body weight reduction of 14.9% at 68 weeks (STEP-1; Wilding et al., NEJM 2021), while tirzepatide demonstrated up to 22.5% weight loss in the SURMOUNT-1 trial (Jastreboff et al., NEJM 2022).

These are powerful, injectable prescription medications classified as controlled therapeutic agents by the HPRA (Ireland) and MHRA (UK). They are not nutritional supplements, lifestyle aids, or cosmetic treatments. Their use must be embedded within structured clinical management.

Established Contraindications and Safety Signals

All GLP-1 RAs carry a black-box warning from the FDA and class warning from the EMA regarding thyroid C-cell tumours observed in rodent studies. Although causality in humans has not been definitively established, prescribers are required to screen for:

  • Personal or family history of medullary thyroid carcinoma (MTC)
  • Multiple Endocrine Neoplasia syndrome type 2 (MEN-2)
  • Active or history of pancreatitis
  • Severe gastrointestinal disease
  • Pregnancy or planned conception
  • Diabetic retinopathy (rapid glycaemic improvement may worsen retinopathy acutely)

Post-marketing surveillance data published in JAMA Internal Medicine (2024) identified a significantly elevated risk of non-arteritic anterior ischaemic optic neuropathy (NAION) in semaglutide users â a finding now under active review by the FDA.

The Drug Shortage Crisis: A Direct Consequence of Unregulated Prescribing

The global demand surge driven by cosmetic weight-loss use has created critical shortages of semaglutide and liraglutide, directly depriving patients with type 2 diabetes and established cardiovascular risk of their first-line, evidence-based therapies. The HPRA and HSE Ireland have issued repeated supply alerts since 2022. The NHS in the UK has prioritised supply for diabetes management over obesity indications.

Clinicians and patients must understand that obtaining GLP-1 RAs through private online portals for cosmetic weight loss directly displaces supply from those with the greatest clinical need.

Key Safety Warnings

  • Unverified online platforms cannot perform the physical examination, baseline bloods (HbA1c, thyroid function, lipid panel, renal function), or screen for contraindications required before initiation.
  • Compounded semaglutide obtained outside licensed pharmacy networks is not bioequivalent to licensed products and lacks quality assurance. The FDA and MHRA have both issued explicit warnings against compounded GLP-1 products.
  • Dose escalation without clinical oversight significantly increases the risk of severe nausea, vomiting, dehydration, gastroparesis, and acute pancreatitis.
  • Abrupt cessation without structured follow-up results in rapid weight regain â typically exceeding baseline weight within 12 months (STEP-4 extension data, 2022).
  • Sarcopenia risk: Without protein-optimised dietary guidance and resistance exercise, GLP-1 RA-induced weight loss includes significant lean muscle mass â a serious concern, particularly in older adults.

What Safe, Regulated Prescribing Looks Like

Guidance from NICE (TA875 â tirzepatide, 2024; TA664 â semaglutide, 2023), the European Association for the Study of Obesity (EASO), and the Irish Society for Clinical Nutrition & Metabolism (IrSCNM) is explicit: GLP-1 RA prescribing for obesity requires:

  • Baseline BMI â¥30 kg/mÂ² (or â¥27 with weight-related comorbidity)
  • Comprehensive metabolic blood panel prior to initiation
  • Contraindication screening by a qualified clinician
  • Structured dose titration schedule with clinical review at each step
  • Dietary and physical activity counselling throughout treatment
  • Regular monitoring â HbA1c, renal function, hepatic enzymes, cardiovascular risk
  • A clear long-term management plan addressing what happens when medication is discontinued

The Cardiometabolic Dimension

The SELECT cardiovascular outcomes trial (Lincoff et al., NEJM 2023) demonstrated that semaglutide 2.4 mg reduced major adverse cardiovascular events (MACE) by 20% in overweight/obese adults with pre-existing cardiovascular disease. This cardioprotective evidence reinforces the case for these agents being prescribed within a combined cardiologyâendocrinology framework â not in isolation by non-specialist platforms.

For patients with both obesity and cardiac risk, integrated care between a Consultant Endocrinologist and Consultant Cardiologist is the gold standard of management.

Frequently Asked Questions

Do I need a GP referral to see a specialist?+

A GP referral is strongly recommended and preferred. It allows us to review your full medical history, current medications, and the clinical reason for referral before your appointment â leading to a more efficient and thorough consultation. However, self-referrals are also accepted. If you are self-referring, please bring any relevant test results, letters, or medical records to your appointment.

How quickly can I get an appointment?+

We aim to offer appointments within 1â4 weeks of receiving a referral, depending on clinical urgency and clinic availability. Urgent referrals are prioritised. Please contact us directly at Syed.Kazmi@healthmail.ie or 089 656 7597 if your situation is time-sensitive.

What conditions do you treat?+

Our endocrinology service covers: Type 1 and Type 2 diabetes, obesity and weight management (including GLP-1 therapies), thyroid disorders (hypothyroidism, hyperthyroidism, nodules), adrenal conditions (Cushing's, Addison's), polycystic ovary syndrome (PCOS), male hypogonadism, pituitary disorders, osteoporosis, and complex lipid disorders.

Our cardiology service covers: chest pain assessment, hypertension, arrhythmia, heart failure, ECG and Holter monitoring, cardiovascular risk assessment, and post-intervention follow-up.

Do you accept VHI, Laya, Irish Life Health, or Aviva?+

Please contact us directly to confirm your specific plan's coverage before attending. Insurer panels and coverage levels change regularly, and we recommend calling your insurer directly to verify whether our consultants are covered under your plan before booking.

Where are you located and is there parking?+

We are located at Suite 5, Vista Primary Care Clinic, Ballymore Road, Naas, Co. Kildare (Eircode: W91 E6H2). Free car parking is available on-site at Vista Primary Care. The clinic is accessible by car from the N7 and is served by Bus Ãireann routes in Naas town.

Can I get a prescription on my first visit?+

This depends entirely on the clinical assessment. In many cases, particularly for diabetes or thyroid conditions where investigations are pending, we may request blood tests and imaging before initiating treatment. However, where the clinical picture is clear, treatment may be initiated at the first consultation. Your consultant will discuss the plan with you at the appointment.

What should I bring to my appointment?+

Please bring: your GP referral letter (if applicable), a list of your current medications, any recent blood test results, relevant imaging or scan reports, your insurance card (if applicable), and a list of any questions you wish to discuss. If you use a continuous glucose monitor (CGM) or insulin pump, please bring the device or access to its data.

Do you treat children, or is this an adults-only service?+

Our clinic primarily serves adult patients (18 years and over). For paediatric endocrinology or cardiology, please discuss with your GP for referral to the appropriate paediatric specialist service.

What is the difference between a consultant and a registrar?+

A consultant is a fully qualified specialist who has completed all postgraduate training and is on the Specialist Register of the Medical Council of Ireland. At our clinic, all consultations are conducted by our named consultants â Dr Kazmi and Dr Kalyar â not by registrars, junior doctors, or locum staff. You will always see the consultant named in your referral.
